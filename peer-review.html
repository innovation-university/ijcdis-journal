Peer review

Proposed double-blind review process, decision criteria, conflicts, and reviewer responsibilities.

These procedures are proposed for the launch and will become operative only after governance, editorial roles, and the submission system are formally activated.

Review model

Research content will undergo editorial screening followed, when suitable, by double-blind peer review from at least two independent reviewers. Author and reviewer identities will be concealed from one another to the extent practically possible.

Workflow

1

Administrative check

Files, declarations, anonymity, scope, and basic completeness are checked.

2

Editorial screening

An editor evaluates fit, originality, methodological credibility, ethics, reporting quality, and likely contribution.

3

Independent review

Suitable manuscripts are sent to reviewers selected for expertise and absence of conflicts.

4

Decision and revision

Decisions may be accept, minor revision, major revision, reject and resubmit, or reject. Acceptance is never guaranteed.

5

Production and proof

Accepted manuscripts undergo metadata, language, figure, reference, and proof checks before publication.

Decision criteria

  • Clear contribution and fit with scope.
  • Appropriate and reproducible methods.
  • Results supported by data and analysis.
  • Transparent limitations and responsible interpretation.
  • Research ethics, legal permissions, and complete declarations.
  • Accurate engagement with relevant literature.

Reviewer responsibilities

Reviewers must preserve confidentiality, decline conflicts, evaluate objectively, identify major methodological and reporting issues, avoid personal criticism, and never use unpublished material for personal advantage.

Editorial conflicts

Editors do not handle papers where they have a personal, financial, academic, or institutional conflict. Editorial-board submissions are assigned to an independent editor and follow the same standards.

Timing

Indicative targets may be published after workflow testing. Any review time is a target rather than a promise; quality and reviewer availability take precedence.
